1

You still need a real prescription, and not everyone gets one

GLP-1 medications are prescription-only. A reputable program has a licensed clinician review your health history, and may order labs, before prescribing. If a site promises approval before anyone looks at your history, treat that as a red flag. Look for programs that refund you if you're not prescribed.

2

"Compounded" and "brand-name" are not the same thing

Brand-name GLP-1s are FDA-approved. Compounded semaglutide or tirzepatide is made by a compounding pharmacy. It's not FDA-approved, and it isn't a generic version of the brand-name drug.2 The FDA has restricted routine compounding now that the brand-name drugs are no longer in shortage. Always ask which one you'd get.

3

The advertised price is usually just the first month

"$39 to start" often means the first month only, or the program fee without the medication. Doses go up over several months, and some prices go up with them. Ask what you'll pay in month four. That's closer to what the program really costs.

A low "starting" price is common. Before you check out, find out whether medication is included and what the price is once your dose increases. A convenient program with surprise costs isn't convenient.

4

Insurance might cover it, or might not

Coverage for weight-management medication depends on your plan and often requires prior authorization. Some programs check your benefits and handle the paperwork for you. Others are cash-pay only. If you have insurance, a program that does the checking can save you real money.

5

Side effects are common at first. Support matters.

Nausea, diarrhea and constipation are the most common side effects, especially when the dose goes up. They often ease over time. Choose a program where you can actually message or see a clinician when something feels off, not just a support inbox.

6

The medication works best alongside habits

In clinical trials, GLP-1s were studied together with a reduced-calorie diet and more physical activity.1 Programs that include nutrition coaching, protein guidance or strength-training tips can help you keep muscle and build routines that last.

7

Check that the company is legit

  • Clinicians licensed in your state, and named on the site
  • A licensed U.S. pharmacy, and the program tells you which one
  • Third-party certification such as LegitScript for telehealth
  • Clear privacy practices for your health information

Important safety information

GLP-1 medications (such as semaglutide and tirzepatide) are available by prescription only and aren't right for everyone. Your clinician decides whether a medication is appropriate for you.

  • Possible thyroid tumors, including cancer. In studies with rodents, these medications caused thyroid C-cell tumors. It isn't known whether they cause these tumors in people. Don't use them if you or a family member has had medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC), or if you have Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia syndrome type 2 (MEN 2).
  • Common side effects include nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, constipation and stomach pain.
  • Serious side effects can include pancreatitis, gallbladder problems, kidney problems, serious allergic reactions, low blood sugar (especially with other diabetes medicines), increased heart rate, and changes in vision in people with diabetes. Tell your clinician about any mood changes or thoughts of self-harm.
  • Don't use these medications if you're pregnant or planning to become pregnant.

This isn't a complete list. Read the Medication Guide for any medication you're prescribed, and talk to your clinician. Report side effects to the FDA at fda.gov/medwatch or 1-800-FDA-1088.

I'm not lazy, and I wasn't lacking willpower. But somewhere after 40 the weight stopped moving no matter what I ate — and it took me far too long to learn it had almost nothing to do with my diet.

For two years I did everything the experts say: ate less, moved more. It worked in my 30s. After 40 it quietly stopped — and the reason, I've since learned, is one of the most talked-about shifts in how medicine thinks about weight.

The "Eat Less, Move More" Myth

It's the advice your doctor gave you. The advice your trainer repeats. The advice every diet book is built around. And for many people over 40, it quietly stops delivering results — not because they're doing it wrong, but because something underneath has changed.

People eating carefully and walking every day, still watching the middle expand. The math doesn't add up — which means something other than calories is driving it.

The Real Reason: A Hormone Called GLP-1

Much of it comes down to a hormone your gut produces every time you eat: GLP-1 (glucagon-like peptide-1). It does three things at once — and all three tend to weaken with age.

What GLP-1 does
3-in-1

Tells your brain you're full · slows digestion so blood sugar doesn't spike · signals your body to release stored fat for energy.

In your 30s, your body responds to this appetite-and-metabolism signal at full strength. Later, that response fades. So you can eat less and still feel hungry, and the stubborn fat around the middle simply sits there. It isn't a willpower problem — it's biology. And until recently, the only medical option that targeted this pathway cost a small fortune.

The $1,200 Problem

You've seen the headlines: Ozempic®, Wegovy®, Mounjaro® — the GLP-1 medications (semaglutide and tirzepatide) that work directly on this pathway. The clinical results behind them are real. The problem was never whether they work. The problem was getting them:

    Hassle: for many people, it simply wasn't worth the time, the denials, or the price.

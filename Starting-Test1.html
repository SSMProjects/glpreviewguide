Why the Weight Won't Budge — And Why It Was Never About Discipline

If you've been told stubborn weight is just a matter of "eating less and moving more," you've been given half the story.

There's a reason the hunger never truly switches off. A reason you can finish a proper meal and feel like you haven't eaten twenty minutes later. A reason the cravings get loudest at night — right after the day you were most careful.

It traces back to a single enzyme your own body makes, called DPP-4.

And once you see what it's been doing behind the scenes, the whole struggle suddenly makes sense.

What Is DPP-4 — And How Is It Quietly Working Against You?

📄 Peer-reviewed research · National Library of Medicine

By now you've almost certainly seen the ads for GLP-1 — the hormone that tells your brain you're satisfied and it's time to stop eating.

Here's the part the ads skip: your gut also releases an enzyme called DPP-4 that breaks GLP-1 down almost the instant it appears — often within a couple of minutes — before the "I'm full" message ever lands.

Picture sending a text that reads "I'm full" — only to have it intercepted and deleted every single time before it reaches your brain.

Your brain never receives the signal. So it assumes the tank is still empty and keeps the hunger dial turned all the way up.

Call it the DPP-4 hunger loop.

And here's the cruel twist: in many women carrying extra weight, DPP-4 runs hotter than normal — so the signal gets cut off faster, and more often.

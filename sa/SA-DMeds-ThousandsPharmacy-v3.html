You're not lazy, and you're not lacking willpower. If the weight stopped moving somewhere after 40 no matter what you eat, it may have far less to do with your diet than you've been told.

Plenty of people do everything the experts say: eat less, move more. It works in their 30s. After 40 it quietly stops, and the reason is one of the most talked-about shifts in how medicine thinks about weight.

The "Eat Less, Move More" Myth

It's the advice your doctor gave you. The advice your trainer repeats. The advice every diet book is built around. And for many people over 40, it quietly stops delivering results — not because they're doing it wrong, but because something underneath has changed.

People eating carefully and walking every day, still watching the middle expand. The math doesn't add up — which means something other than calories is driving it.

The Real Reason: A Hormone Called GLP-1

Much of it comes down to a hormone your gut produces every time you eat: GLP-1 (glucagon-like peptide-1). It does three things at once — and all three tend to weaken with age.

What GLP-1 does
3-in-1

Tells your brain you're full · slows digestion so blood sugar doesn't spike · signals your body to release stored fat for energy.

In your 30s, your body responds to this appetite-and-metabolism signal at full strength. Later, that response fades. So you can eat less and still feel hungry, and the stubborn fat around the middle simply sits there. It isn't a willpower problem — it's biology. And until recently, the only medical option that targeted this pathway cost a small fortune.

The $1,200 Problem

You've seen the headlines about GLP-1 medications, which work directly on this pathway. The problem was getting them:

  • ✕
    Cost: $900–$1,400 a month out of pocket. Most insurance plans deny coverage for weight loss.
  • ✕
    Access: in-person appointments, long waits, and pharmacy shortages reported since 2023.
  • ✕
    Hassle: for many people, it simply wasn't worth the time, the denials, or the price.

For a lot of people — especially those on fixed incomes, or without weight-loss coverage — that price put the whole thing out of reach. So a different route has opened up.

Why You Haven't Heard More About This

Part of it is simply that telehealth access like this is new, and word travels slowly. And part of it is that the traditional route — brand-name medications filled at retail pharmacies — is a very large, very established business. There isn't much incentive to point people toward a cheaper path. So most people only hear about it from a friend who already tried it.

Insurance help

Ro checks your coverage for Ozempic, Wegovy and Zepbound pens for free and handles the paperwork for you, including prior authorization if your plan needs it. You can run the check at any time, and you can pay cash instead if you aren't covered. Coverage varies by plan and diagnosis, and approval isn't guaranteed.

Questions people ask

How much does Ro's weight loss program cost?

The Ro Body membership is $39 for the first month, then $74 to $149 a month depending on plan length. Medication is billed separately, from $149 a month for the Wegovy pill or Foundayo pill, $199 for the Wegovy pen and $299 for the Zepbound KwikPen at cash-pay starting prices.

Does Ro offer compounded semaglutide or tirzepatide?

No. Ro offers FDA-approved brand-name GLP-1 medications only. If you want compounded options, see our cost comparison.

Does Ro take insurance?

Ro checks your coverage for Ozempic, Wegovy and Zepbound pens for free and handles the paperwork. Coverage isn't guaranteed, and you can also pay cash.

What happens if I'm not eligible?

Ro refunds the $39 first-month membership if you aren't eligible for GLP-1s. A licensed clinician decides eligibility.

Is the membership included in the medication price?

No. Ro bills two separate charges: the membership and the medication. Add both together to get your monthly cost.

Important safety information

GLP-1 medications are available only through a licensed clinician and aren't right for everyone. Your clinician decides whether a medication is appropriate for you.

  • Possible thyroid tumors, including cancer. In studies with rodents, these medications caused thyroid C-cell tumors. It isn't known whether they cause these tumors in people. Don't use them if you or a family member has had medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC), or if you have Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia syndrome type 2 (MEN 2).
  • Common side effects include nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, constipation and stomach pain.
  • Serious side effects can include pancreatitis, gallbladder problems, kidney problems, serious allergic reactions, low blood sugar (especially with other diabetes medicines), increased heart rate, and vision changes in people with diabetes. Tell your clinician about mood changes or thoughts of self-harm.
  • Don't use these medications if you're pregnant or planning to become pregnant.

This isn't a complete list. Read the Medication Guide for any medication you're prescribed. Report side effects to the FDA at fda.gov/medwatch or 1-800-FDA-1088.

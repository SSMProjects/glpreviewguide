What changes the total cost

Membership plus medication, or one plan price

Ro charges a membership and bills medication separately. TrimRx, Embody, Trimi and Pallas Health don't charge a membership fee, and Trimi and Pallas include visits and follow-ups in the plan price. Compare the total of everything you'll be charged each month, not the headline medication price.

Brand-name or compounded

Brand-name medications such as Wegovy and Zepbound are FDA-approved and usually cost more without insurance. Compounded versions are prepared by compounding pharmacies, are not FDA-approved and generally cost less. Ask any provider exactly which medication and pharmacy they use.

First-month offers and renewals

Most programs discount the first month. Wellorithm, for example, charges $179 for the first month of compounded semaglutide and $299 every month after that. Look at the renewal price, because that's what you'll pay for most of your treatment.

Monthly versus every 4 weeks

Pallas Health bills every 4 weeks, which works out to 13 charges a year. Prepaid plans (Ro, Trimi, Pallas's annual plan) lower the monthly equivalent but require paying for several months up front.

Dose

Some programs keep the same price across doses (Wellorithm says so for its compounded plans). With brand-name medications, the price usually rises as the dose increases.

Refunds if you aren't eligible

Ro refunds the $39 first month, MEDGm and Trimi offer full refunds, and Pallas Health charges only once a clinician prescribes. Wellorithm doesn't charge for medication if you aren't prescribed, but may keep the consultation fee.

Questions people ask

Why is Ro more expensive than compounded programs?

Ro offers FDA-approved brand-name medications only and charges a membership on top of the medication. Compounded programs price medication, visits and shipping together, usually for less, but compounded drugs are not FDA-approved.

What does “every 4 weeks” billing mean for my yearly cost?

Plans billed every 4 weeks charge 13 times a year, not 12. Multiply the 4-week price by 13 to compare it with a monthly price.

Are the holiday prices permanent?

Not necessarily. TrimRx and Embody were advertising holiday promotions on November 16, 2026. Check the price and plan terms at checkout before you pay.

Does a higher dose cost more?

It depends on the program. Wellorithm says its compounded price is the same across the dose range, and Pallas Health and Trimi publish flat plan prices. Ro's brand-name medication prices rise with the dose.

Can I use HSA or FSA funds?

MEDGm and Pallas Health say their plans are HSA/FSA eligible. Eligibility depends on your plan; check with your HSA/FSA administrator.

Will insurance pay for a GLP-1?

Coverage varies by plan and diagnosis. Ro checks coverage for Ozempic, Wegovy and Zepbound pens for free and handles the paperwork, and Wellorithm offers brand-name care through insurance for a $49 monthly service fee. Approval is never guaranteed.

Important safety information

GLP-1 medications are available only through a licensed clinician and aren't right for everyone. Your clinician decides whether a medication is appropriate for you.

  • Possible thyroid tumors, including cancer. In studies with rodents, these medications caused thyroid C-cell tumors. It isn't known whether they cause these tumors in people. Don't use them if you or a family member has had medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC), or if you have Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia syndrome type 2 (MEN 2).
  • Common side effects include nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, constipation and stomach pain.
  • Serious side effects can include pancreatitis, gallbladder problems, kidney problems, serious allergic reactions, low blood sugar (especially with other diabetes medicines), increased heart rate, and vision changes in people with diabetes. Tell your clinician about mood changes or thoughts of self-harm.
  • Don't use these medications if you're pregnant or planning to become pregnant.

This isn't a complete list. Read the Medication Guide for any medication you're prescribed. Report side effects to the FDA at fda.gov/medwatch or 1-800-FDA-1088.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need clinician approval?

Yes. GLP-1 medications are available only through a licensed clinician. Each program has a licensed clinician review your health history, and they may decide a GLP-1 isn't right for you.

Are compounded GLP-1s the same as brand-name medications?

No. Compounded GLP-1 medications are not FDA-approved and are not generic versions of brand-name drugs. Ask any program exactly which medication and pharmacy it uses.

Will insurance cover it?

Coverage for weight-management medication varies widely by plan, and no program can promise it. Ro offers a free insurance check for some brand-name medications, and Wellorithm offers an insurance-based brand-name path with a monthly service fee. MEDGm and Pallas Health accept HSA/FSA.

How much do online GLP-1 programs cost?

It depends on the medication, the dose, the plan length and whether membership and medication are billed separately. On this page, published first payments range from $39 (Ro membership, medication extra) to $179 (Wellorithm), and ongoing charges range from a $99/mo equivalent on Trimi’s prepaid plans to $299/mo or more. Always compare the first charge, the renewal charge and how often it’s billed. Brand-name drugs usually cost more.

What side effects are common?

Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea and constipation are the most common, especially when the dose increases. See the safety information below and talk to your clinician.

How long does it take to get started?

Most programs start with a short online intake. A licensed provider then reviews it; timing varies by program, and approved medication typically ships within a few days.

What is a GLP-1?

GLP-1 receptor agonists are medications that act like a natural hormone involved in appetite and blood sugar. Some are FDA-approved for chronic weight management in adults who meet specific criteria, and others are approved for type 2 diabetes. They're used together with a reduced-calorie diet and more physical activity.

Online programs connect you with a licensed clinician who reviews your health history, decides whether a GLP-1 is appropriate and, if so, prescribes either an FDA-approved brand-name medication or a compounded version.

Brand-name vs. compounded

Brand-name vs. compounded

Brand-name GLP-1s have been through FDA review for safety and effectiveness. Compounded versions are made by licensed compounding pharmacies, aren't FDA-approved and aren't generic versions of brand-name drugs.

Before you choose

Ask which medication you'd get, which pharmacy prepares it, what you'll pay as your dose increases, and how to cancel. Some brand-name options are FDA-approved for type 2 diabetes rather than weight loss.

Important safety information

GLP-1 medications are available only through a licensed clinician and aren't right for everyone. Your clinician decides whether a medication is appropriate for you.

  • Possible thyroid tumors, including cancer. In studies with rodents, these medications caused thyroid C-cell tumors. It isn't known whether they cause these tumors in people. Don't use them if you or a family member has had medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC), or if you have Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia syndrome type 2 (MEN 2).
  • Common side effects include nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, constipation and stomach pain.
  • Serious side effects can include pancreatitis, gallbladder problems, kidney problems, serious allergic reactions, low blood sugar (especially with other diabetes medicines), increased heart rate, and vision changes in people with diabetes. Tell your clinician about mood changes or thoughts of self-harm.
  • Don't use these medications if you're pregnant or planning to become pregnant.

This isn't a complete list. Read the Medication Guide for any medication you're prescribed. Report side effects to the FDA at fda.gov/medwatch or 1-800-FDA-1088.

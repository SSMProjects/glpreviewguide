GLP-1 medicines have gone mainstream: in a 2024 KFF poll, about 1 in 8 U.S. adults said they had taken one. In 2026, the FDA approved once-daily GLP-1 pills for weight management, so a needle is no longer the only option.

Whether you want to lose 15 pounds or 60, your goal matters. It shapes what a clinician recommends and what your plan costs. Pick yours above, or keep reading.

How GLP-1s fit into a weight-loss plan

Your gut releases a hormone called GLP-1 after you eat. GLP-1 medicines act on that same signal, more strongly and for longer. Along with a reduced-calorie diet and more activity, that can mean:

  • 1
    Feeling full sooner and staying satisfied with smaller portions.
  • 2
    Slower stomach emptying, so meals last longer.
  • 3
    Fewer cravings between meals, with steadier blood sugar.
  • 4
    Less "food noise": many people describe the constant thoughts about food getting quieter.

In the main trial for one of the approved pills, adults taking it for 64 weeks alongside diet and exercise lost 13.6% of their body weight on average, compared with 2.2% on placebo. Results vary, and stomach side effects were common.

Who may qualify

Prescription GLP-1 treatment may be appropriate for adults who:

  • Have a BMI of 30 or higher, or
  • Have a BMI of 27 or higher plus a weight-related condition, such as high blood pressure or high cholesterol
  • Have tried lifestyle changes alone and want a medically supervised approach

A licensed clinician makes the final call after reviewing your health history. People with a personal or family history of certain thyroid cancers, and people who are pregnant, shouldn't use these medicines.

Questions people ask

How fast will I lose weight?

It's gradual. Doses start low and increase over several weeks, and in trials most of the weight loss happened over months. Your clinician will set expectations for your situation.

Will I have to take it forever?

Not necessarily, but many people regain weight after stopping. Ask your clinician about a long-term plan.

Do I need a prescription?

Yes. Every GLP-1 medication, pill or shot, requires a prescription. Online programs connect you with a licensed clinician who reviews your health history and decides whether a prescription is appropriate.

Important safety information

GLP-1 medications are available only with a prescription from a licensed clinician and aren't right for everyone.

  • Possible thyroid tumors, including cancer. In studies with rodents, GLP-1 medicines like these caused thyroid C-cell tumors. It isn't known whether they cause these tumors in people. Don't use them if you or a family member has had medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC), or if you have Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia syndrome type 2 (MEN 2).
  • Common side effects include nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, constipation, stomach pain, headache and tiredness.
  • Serious side effects can include pancreatitis, gallbladder problems, kidney problems, serious allergic reactions, low blood sugar (especially with insulin or sulfonylureas), increased heart rate, vision changes in people with type 2 diabetes, and depression or thoughts of self-harm. Tell your clinician right away about any of these.
  • Don't use these medications if you're pregnant or planning to become pregnant.
  • Pills must be taken exactly as directed; some must be taken on an empty stomach with a small amount of water.

This isn't a complete list. Read the Medication Guide for any medication you're prescribed. Report side effects to the FDA at fda.gov/medwatch or 1-800-FDA-1088.

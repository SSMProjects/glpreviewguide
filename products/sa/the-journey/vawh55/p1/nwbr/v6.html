What actually changes after menopause

This isn't vanity, and it isn't in your head. As estrogen falls around menopause, the body tends to store more fat around the abdomen, including deeper "visceral" fat around the organs, instead of on the hips and thighs. Many women also lose some muscle with age, which lowers the calories they burn at rest, and poor sleep during the transition can make hunger harder to manage.

Put those together and the math changes: the same diet that worked at 35 produces less at 55, and the weight that does come on tends to go to your middle.

If you were told to "just eat less"

Plenty of women ask about weight-loss medication and are told to keep trying diet and exercise. Sometimes that's the right advice. But GLP-1 medicines are FDA-approved for adults with a BMI of 30 or higher, or 27 or higher with a weight-related condition such as high blood pressure or high cholesterol. If that describes you, it's reasonable to ask for a second opinion.

How GLP-1 medicines work on hunger

GLP-1 is a hormone your gut releases when you eat. It helps signal fullness to your brain and slows how quickly your stomach empties. GLP-1 medicines work on that same pathway, for longer. Many people say the constant thinking about food, often called "food noise," gets quieter, and smaller portions feel like enough.

Now there's a pill

Until recently, every FDA-approved GLP-1 medication for weight loss was an injection. Then the FDA approved the first once-daily GLP-1 pill for chronic weight management, which became available in January 2026. A second, different GLP-1 pill was approved in April 2026.

Both are brand-name medicines made by the drugmakers and approved by the FDA. They're not compounded copies. They work on the same pathway as the weekly shots: GLP-1, a hormone your gut releases when you eat that helps signal fullness and slows digestion.

What the trial found, including for women

In the main clinical trial for one of the approved pills, 307 adults with overweight or obesity took the pill or a placebo daily for 64 weeks, with diet and exercise guidance. Most participants were women (79%), and the average age was 48. On average:

Questions people ask

Does a GLP-1 work after menopause?

These medicines were studied in adults of many ages; in the pill trial above, most participants were women and the average age was 48. A clinician can tell you whether it's appropriate with your health history and any hormone therapy you take.

Can I take it with hormone therapy?

Tell your clinician about every medication you take, including hormone therapy, so they can check for interactions.

Important safety information

GLP-1 medications are available only with a prescription from a licensed clinician and aren't right for everyone.

  • Possible thyroid tumors, including cancer. In studies with rodents, GLP-1 medicines like these caused thyroid C-cell tumors. It isn't known whether they cause these tumors in people. Don't use them if you or a family member has had medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC), or if you have Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia syndrome type 2 (MEN 2).
  • Common side effects include nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, constipation, stomach pain, headache and tiredness.
  • Serious side effects can include pancreatitis, gallbladder problems, kidney problems, serious allergic reactions, low blood sugar (especially with insulin or sulfonylureas), increased heart rate, vision changes in people with type 2 diabetes, and depression or thoughts of self-harm. Tell your clinician right away about any of these.
  • Don't use these medications if you're pregnant or planning to become pregnant.
  • Pills must be taken exactly as directed; some must be taken on an empty stomach with a small amount of water.

This isn't a complete list. Read the Medication Guide for any medication you're prescribed. Report side effects to the FDA at fda.gov/medwatch or 1-800-FDA-1088.

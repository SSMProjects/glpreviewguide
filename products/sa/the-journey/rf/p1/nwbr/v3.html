Compounded, not FDA-approved: what that means

Through Refills, licensed providers prescribe compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide. Compounded medicines are made by licensed pharmacies for individual patients. They are not approved or evaluated for safety, effectiveness or quality by the FDA, and they are not the same as brand-name GLP-1 medicines.

The large weight-loss studies you may have read about were done with FDA-approved brand-name products, so their results can't be assumed for compounded versions. That's one reason the lower price exists, and it's worth weighing before you start.

Who may qualify

GLP-1 treatment is usually considered for adults who:

  • Have a BMI of 30 or higher, or
  • Have a BMI of 27 or higher plus a weight-related condition, such as high blood pressure or high cholesterol

A licensed provider makes the call after reviewing your health history. People with a personal or family history of certain thyroid cancers, and people who are pregnant, shouldn't use these medicines.

Important safety information

Compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide are available only with a prescription from a licensed provider and aren't right for everyone. Compounded drug products are not approved or evaluated for safety, effectiveness or quality by the FDA.

  • Possible thyroid tumors, including cancer. In studies with rodents, semaglutide and tirzepatide caused thyroid C-cell tumors. It isn't known whether they cause these tumors in people. Don't use them if you or a family member has had medullary thyroid carcinoma or if you have Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia syndrome type 2 (MEN 2).
  • Common side effects include nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, constipation, stomach pain, decreased appetite, headache and tiredness.
  • Serious side effects can include pancreatitis, gallbladder problems, kidney problems, serious allergic reactions, low blood sugar (especially with insulin or sulfonylureas), increased heart rate, and depression or thoughts of self-harm.
  • Measure and take your dose exactly as your provider directs. The FDA has received reports of dosing errors with compounded GLP-1 injections.
  • Don't use these medications if you're pregnant or planning to become pregnant.

This isn't a complete list. Ask your provider about all risks. Report side effects to the FDA at fda.gov/medwatch or 1-800-FDA-1088.
